For the grown-ups

This book has two parts. The story follows Malak and Yousef through one summer with a juice stand. Each scene ends with one of Grandpa's ideas about money: cost, price, profit, saving, honesty and rest.

The second part shows how to play The Little Entrepreneur, the free game the story comes from. It works in any browser and on phones, with no ads, no accounts and nothing to buy.

A good way to use it: read one scene together, talk about Grandpa's idea, then let your child try it in the game. At the end there is a page for planning a real tiny business.

Part 1 · 1

Summer begins

On the first day of summer, Malak and Yousef had a plan. "Mum's birthday is at the end of summer," said Malak. "We want to buy her a real gift." Yousef emptied his pockets: 15 coins. "That's not enough," he sighed. Grandpa put down his tea. "Then let's earn the rest. What do people here want on a hot day?" "Something cold!" they shouted together.

Grandpa's idea

A business starts with something people want.

Part 1 · 2

What does one cup cost?

Grandpa opened his old notebook. "Lemons, sugar, water and a paper cup. Let's count." They added it all up and divided it by the number of cups. "One cup costs us one coin to make," said Yousef. "That is our cost," said Grandpa. "We pay for every cup we make, even if nobody buys it."

Grandpa's idea

Cost is what you pay to make something.

5
Part 1 · 3

Choosing a price

Two, 3 or 5 coins a cup? "5!" said Yousef. "More money!" Malak shook her head. "At 5, fewer people will stop. At two coins lots will come, but we only keep one coin from each cup." Grandpa smiled. "Try 3. It is fair for them, and fair for you." They wrote it on the sign in big letters: 3 coins.

Grandpa's idea

A high price brings fewer customers. A low price brings less profit per cup.

Part 1 · 4

Market day!

On Saturday they made 12 cups. The sun was out, and so were the neighbours. By noon, 11 cups were gone. Yousef counted the coins twice: 33 coins. "We're rich!" he cheered. "Not so fast," said Grandpa. "How much did the cups cost?" "12 coins." "So what is left?"

Grandpa's idea

Counting the coins that came in is only half of the story.

Malak wrote it on the back of the sign. 33 coins came in. 12 coins went out. 21 coins are profit. "Profit is what's left after paying for everything," said Grandpa. Yousef put the 21 coins in the piggy bank very slowly, one by one.

Grandpa's idea

Profit = coins in − coins spent.

8
Part 1 · 6

A rainy Saturday

The next Saturday they made 20 cups. Then the clouds came. Only 6 people stopped, and they wanted something warm. That evening, 14 cups of juice went down the sink. "We paid for those," said Yousef quietly. "Next time," said Malak, "we look at the sky first, and make about as many as people will buy."

Grandpa's idea

Leftovers that spoil are coins thrown in the bin. Making fewer is a skill.

9
Part 1 · 7

Too sour!

Uncle Saeed took one sip and made a face. "Too sour!" he said, loud enough for the whole line to hear. Yousef's cheeks went red. Malak poured a new cup with more sugar. "Can you tell us if this one is better?" He tried it, nodded, and told the line, "Now it's perfect." That afternoon they changed the recipe for good.

Grandpa's idea

A complaint is free advice.

10
Part 1 · 8

6 extra coins

At closing time, the box had 6 coins too many. "The lady with the green basket paid too much," said Malak. "Nobody will know," said Yousef. Malak looked at him. Yousef sighed, and they ran after her down the street. The lady laughed. "Nobody has ever chased me to give money back!" The next Saturday she came back with two friends.

Grandpa's idea

Trust is like savings: slow to build, quick to spend.

11
Part 1 · 9

The stand next door

One morning a bigger boy called Karim set up a juice stand 5 steps away. Same sign, same cups, same price. "He copied us!" said Yousef. Grandpa sipped his tea. "Then ask yourselves what makes you different." Malak had an idea. She walked over and talked to Karim. Now Karim sells popcorn, they sell juice, and people buy both.

Grandpa's idea

When someone copies you, the question is: what makes you, you?

12
Part 1 · 10

Too tired to count

In week 6, Malak and Yousef stayed up late making 30 cups. The next day their eyes kept closing. Yousef gave a customer 5 coins change instead of two. Malak forgot the ice. "Tired sellers make small mistakes," said Grandpa. "Rest is part of the job." So on Sunday they played football, did their homework and slept early.

Grandpa's idea

Rest is part of the job.

13
Part 1 · 11

The new game

The piggy bank was getting heavy. Then everyone at the summer club started talking about a new video game. It cost 30 coins. "We have enough," whispered Yousef. Malak looked at the piggy bank, then at Mum's photo on the fridge. They waited. 3 weeks later, the same game was on sale for 18 coins. They smiled, and kept saving.

Grandpa's idea

Saving means picking what you want most over what you want right now.

Part 1 · 12

The last day of summer

On the last Saturday, Yousef opened the piggy bank. They counted every coin twice: 236 coins. The gift for Mum cost 220. "We did it!" they cheered. Mum cried happy tears when she opened it. Grandpa gave them a sticker each. "You didn't just earn coins," he said. "You learned how a business works." Malak was already planning next summer.

Grandpa's idea

Every coin you don't spend today walks your goal one step closer.

Part 2

How to play The Little Entrepreneur

In the game you run your own tiny business for a 12-week summer, just like Malak and Yousef. Pick a business, set a savings goal, and make one plan a week.

Play free at adam.ahmedhaz.com/little in any browser. It works offline once opened, in Arabic and English. A summer takes about 15 minutes.

Part 2 · 1

Start a summer

  1. Type your first name. It stays on your device.
  2. Pick your picture.
  3. Pick a business: Juice stand, Drawings & bracelets, Bike wash & fix, or Cakes & cookies.
  4. Pick a goal: a football (100), a book set (160), a gift for Mum (220) or a new bike (300).
19
Part 2 · 2

Your three meters

  1. Coins: everything you have. Making things costs coins, selling brings them back.
  2. Energy: selling and staying up late use it, rest brings it back. Under 40 you get sleepy. At 70 or more, a wise third choice opens.
  3. Smiles: how much customers like your stand. Fair prices and good quality raise it.
  4. The piggy bank bar shows how close you are to your goal.
20
Part 2 · 3

Plan your week

  1. Check the market day weather. Hot days bring more juice customers, rain brings fewer.
  2. Choose a price: cheap, fair or high.
  3. Choose how many to make. Grandpa guesses how many customers will come.
  4. Choose one activity: Make a poster, Practise, Study, or Rest & play.
21
Part 2 · 4

Market day and results

  1. Watch the customers arrive, or tap Skip.
  2. Read the results: coins in, coins spent, and the profit.
  3. Juice and cakes spoil. Drawings and wash kits are kept for next week.
  4. Spend a few coins on a treat, or save them in the piggy bank.
22
Part 2 · 5

Dilemmas and the wise door

  1. Most weeks bring a small dilemma with two fair answers. Each one costs something.
  2. With 70 energy or more, a third door opens: the wise door.
  3. After each choice, Grandpa shares an idea, and it goes into your sticker book.
23
Part 2 · 6

Echoes, homework and sleepy days

  1. Some choices come back weeks later, with the week they came from.
  2. Study at least once every 3 weeks, or Mum or Dad closes the stand for a week.
  3. Under 40 energy, the numbers wobble and change gets miscounted. Rest fixes it.
24
Part 2 · 7

The end of summer

  1. Reach your goal by week 12 to win it.
  2. Your report card gives stars for Money, Customers, Balance and Honesty.
  3. No harsh endings. If you don't make it, Grandpa sums up what you learned and offers another summer.

Grandpa's tips for a good summer

  • Make about as many as Grandpa guesses.
  • A fair price is a good place to start.
  • Rest before energy drops under 40.
  • Study at least once every 3 weeks.
  • Treats are fine. Just not every week.

Words to know

Cost

What you pay to make something.

Price

What a customer pays you for it.

Profit

Coins in minus coins spent.

Customer

Someone who buys from you.

Leftovers

Things you made but didn't sell.

Saving

Keeping coins now for something bigger later.

Investing

Spending coins on something that helps you earn more.

Partner

Someone who shares the work and the profit.
